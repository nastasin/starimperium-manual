Goal & Winning 01 / 12

Each player defends a planet that starts at 25 health. You win the instant any of these happens:

  • Enemy planet destroyed — reduce your opponent's planet to 0. Ships alone can only grind a planet down to 1; to land the killing blow your assault must include infantry — a soldier attacking the planet, or a ship carrying boarded soldiers.
  • Ascension — raise your own planet to 50 (some cards heal your planet).
  • Deck-out — your opponent has no cards left to draw.
  • Concede — your opponent gives up.
  • Turn limit — if the game runs very long and neither planet falls, it ends at the turn cap: in a campaign siege the attacker loses, and an even match is a draw.

Protect your own planet while pushing the enemy's toward 0.

The Turn 02 / 12

Players alternate turns. At the start of your turn, automatically: draw a card (the very first player skips their first draw), refill your resource pools, regenerate your units' shields, and ready your units — clearing the spent state on any that attacked last turn — so they can act again.

Then, in any order, you may:

  • Play one resource card.
  • Deploy ships, soldiers, and forces you can afford.
  • Attack with your ready units — both ships and soldiers.

Newly played units are summoning sick — they can't attack the turn they arrive. Press End Turn when you're done. Your hand holds up to 8 cards and the board holds up to 8 units.

Resources 03 / 12

There are five resource colors: Anti-Matter, Light, Crystals, Metals, and Organics. Every non-resource card costs an amount of a single color.

  • Resource cards are free to play — but only one per turn, so your economy grows over several turns.
  • Playing a resource adds to that color's pool; pools refill each turn.
  • To play a Ship, Soldier, Force, or Action you need enough of its color in your pool.

On your turn, cards you can afford (and have room for) are highlighted.

Card Types 04 / 12

  • Ship — your main combatants; most can attack and block. A capital ship (any Ship that isn't a support craft) can be boarded and fights other ships and planets, but can't attack soldiers; smaller support ships share the ground front and can engage soldiers too.
  • Soldier — ground troops. They attack enemy soldiers and the planet on their own; attacking enemy ships needs the Can Attack Ships ability. Many carry special abilities; boarding soldiers can join a capital ship.
  • Force — stays in play and applies a continuous effect while it's out (e.g. +Power/+Health to your units). Lose it and the bonus disappears. A Force isn't permanent: it loses 1 health at the start of each of your turns and is destroyed at 0, so its lifespan is its health. And only one Force can be in play at a time — playing any Force (by either player) destroys the one already on the table.
  • Action — a one-shot effect that resolves immediately, then goes to the graveyard.
  • Resource — your economy (see above).

Ships, soldiers, and forces each take one of your 8 board slots; actions and resources don't.

Combat 05 / 12

Attack with a ready unit by targeting an enemy unit or the enemy planet. An attack always lands in full on its target — you can't dodge or redirect the hit itself.

  • Return fire — when you attack a unit, it shoots back at your attacker in the same exchange (and the defender may commit more of their units to pile on), so a unit fight is a mutual trade. Protector units can only defend, never attack.
  • Defending your planet — a planet attack hits your planet in full unless a Protector intercepts it (only a Protector can body-block a planet attack): the Protector takes the damage itself (your planet takes none, even against overkill) and trades fire with the attacker. Commit several and the first one takes the hit. Defending is optional.
  • Acting on defense — you don't have to wait for your own turn to respond: while defending against an attack you may also play Action cards and activate your own units' abilities right then, resolving immediately.
  • Spent after attacking — a unit that attacks is spent (shown faded) until the start of your next turn: it can't attack or defend again, though it can still be attacked and its abilities keep working.
  • Shields first — damage is soaked by a unit's shield before its health. Shields regenerate at the start of your turn; health damage stays.
  • First strike (Ranged Attack) — a first-striker fires before taking any return fire, so if its shot destroys the target it takes no damage back. If both sides have first strike, they fire simultaneously and both take damage.

A hit only kills if it gets through the shield and meets the health.

Boarding & Graveyard 06 / 12

Boarding — some soldiers have the Board ability. Drag such a soldier onto one of your own capital ships (any Ship that isn't a support craft) and it climbs aboard: it leaves the board and gives that ship +1 Power and +1 Health for as long as it's aboard. Board more soldiers for more bonus. A Kill-in-Transit attacker can pick boarded soldiers off one at a time, and if a boarded ship is destroyed its passengers go down with it.

Graveyard — destroyed cards go to your graveyard (click your deck card to view it). You can Salvage a ship, soldier, or force from your graveyard back onto the board by paying its cost again and having a free board slot.

  • Resources are the exception — a destroyed resource is permanently removed from the match. It never enters the graveyard and can't be salvaged, so losing a resource is a permanent hit to your economy.

Abilities 07 / 12

A card's text lists its abilities; the stats shown on a board unit already include any active buffs.

Combat

  • First Strike (Ranged Attack) — fires before taking return fire; no return damage if the shot kills. If both sides have it, they fire at once and both take damage.
  • Can Attack Ships — lets a soldier attack enemy ships (soldiers can already attack soldiers and the planet).
  • Can Attack Soldiers — lets a ship attack enemy soldiers (capital ships normally can't).
  • Slayer — bonus combat damage against a specific kind of target (e.g. +2 vs Machines).
  • Protector — can block/defend but never attacks; only a Protector can intercept an attack aimed at a friendly soldier or your planet (some protect only while you control a matching unit).
  • Protection — reduces incoming damage from a chosen kind of attacker.
  • Kill in Transit — picks a boarded soldier off an enemy ship, one at a time.
  • Attack Lock — while in play, affected units can't declare attacks (they can still defend).
  • Devour — soldiers this unit destroys are exiled (removed from the game) instead of going to the graveyard, so they can never be salvaged.
  • Unblockable — this unit's attacks can't be blocked; the damage always lands.
  • Ship Defender — a soldier that can attack and defend against ships (soldiers normally can't engage ships at all).
  • Capture on Kill — enemy soldiers this unit destroys switch to your side instead of dying.
  • Board Capture — board your capital ship with this soldier to capture (not just destroy) an enemy capital ship it defeats.
  • Displace — pins a target enemy unit: while this unit is in play (and hasn't attacked), the pinned unit can't attack or defend others.
  • Self-Destruct — when this unit attacks a unit and deals damage, both are destroyed after combat (a kamikaze trade).
  • Open Path — while in play, your ships' planet attacks can't be blocked.

Stealth

  • Cloaking — enters play cloaked (the enemy sees only a card back) and can't be attacked or targeted by the enemy while hidden — only board-wide effects can reach it. The cloak lifts when the unit attacks or defends, then re-cloaks at the end of the turn.
  • Reveal Hidden — reveals the opponent's cloaked units (e.g. Special Forces uncloaks enemy units at the start of your turn).

Units & Economy

  • Boarding — a soldier that climbs aboard a friendly capital ship for +1 Power / +1 Health.
  • Change to Ship — turns one of your soldiers into a ship.
  • Clone — creates a copy of a unit.
  • Spawn — creates temporary token units each turn (they vanish at end of turn).
  • Ready — un-spends a unit so it can act again this turn.
  • Draw — draw extra cards.
  • Resource boost — adds to your resource pool to grow your economy faster.
  • Prospect — look at the top 3 cards of your deck, take a resource card from them, and put the rest on the bottom.
  • Salvage (From Grave) — returns a card from the graveyard to play.
  • Assemble — combine your Constructs into ever-larger machines, building up toward the Colossus.
  • Metamorph — grows a little each turn and, once past a health threshold, transforms into a bigger unit.
  • Morph — immediately transforms a chosen friendly unit into another.
  • Transport — any soldier may board this ship, not only ones with the Board ability.
  • Search (Tutor) — search your deck for a card and take it (sometimes only if a condition is met, e.g. you control 3+ Rigs).
  • Assimilate — sacrifice one of your Constructs to give this unit +1 Power / +1 Health.
  • Resurrection — a boarding parasite that returns to battle when the enemy ship it destroyed goes down, instead of dying with it.

Effects

  • Damage — deals direct damage to a target.
  • Restore Health / Shield — heals a wounded unit or refills its shield.
  • Planet Health — heals your planet (toward Ascension) or damages the enemy's.
  • Infect — a decay effect: the infected unit loses health every turn.
  • Doom — marks a unit to be destroyed at the end of the turn.
  • Take Control — steal an enemy unit (e.g. seize an enemy capital ship).
  • Chaos — a random, disruptive effect.
  • Sacrifice — forces a player to give up their weakest unit each turn.
  • Cull — forces a player to give up their weakest soldier each turn.
  • Death Discharge — when this unit dies, it deals damage to the enemy planet.
  • Action Immune — this unit ignores hostile effects that come from Action cards.
  • Seal Planet — while in play, a planet can't gain health — shuts down enemy Ascension healing.
  • Enemy Sacrifice — forces the opponent to give up a card of their choice (e.g. after this unit successfully attacks a planet).
  • Reveal — reveals the enemy's hand.

Control & Disruption

  • Erase — exiles a target enemy unit (or a card from their graveyard): it's removed from the game for good and can never return, not even by salvage.
  • Silence — shuts off a target enemy unit's abilities for as long as the silencing card stays in play; remove the source and the abilities come back.
  • Mill — each turn, exiles cards from the top of the enemy's deck, pushing them toward a deck-out loss.
  • Deck Erase (Null Field) — each turn, the affected player exiles the top card of their own deck, grinding them toward a deck-out.
  • Suppress Reload — while in play, resources don't reload at the start of the turn.

Forces & Passives

  • Force buffs (Power / Health / Shield) — a Force that continuously boosts your units while it's in play; some grow the bonus each turn.
  • Salvage Discount — a Force that halves the cost to salvage matching cards from the graveyard.
  • Banding — units that share this card's name reinforce each other's health.
  • Resilience — immune to Force stat effects (Force +/− Power/Health/Shield never apply to it).

Deck Building 08 / 12

Open Deck Builder from the main menu to build and edit decks.

A deck is battle-ready — and can be taken into a match — only when it meets all of these:

  • Exactly 50 cards.
  • At most 4 copies of any non-resource card (resource cards have no per-card limit — stack your economy).
  • At most one ship Champion and one soldier Champion (a single copy of each) — Champions are singletons, one per combat layer.
  • At most 2 Force cards total (all Forces counted together, not per name) — they're powerful global effects, and only one Force can be in play at a time anyway.
  • Only cards you own.

A deck that breaks any of these can't be selected on the Pre-Battle screen. Decks are also color-themed — lean into one or two resource colors so your cards' costs match what your resources produce.

Pick your deck before a match on the Pre-Battle screen.

Collection & Store 09 / 12

Your collection is every card you own — the pool you build decks from. It grows and shrinks outside of battle.

Earning Cards

  • Conquest — the first time you capture a campaign planet, you gain copies of its color's resource card (more for higher-rated worlds).
  • First win of the day — your first victory each day pays a bonus of your faction's resource cards.
  • Daily mission — completing the day's target grants a random non-resource card of that planet's color, favoring ones you don't own yet.
  • Store — Buy — spend resource cards on a pack (random cards, one guaranteed rare) or a full starter deck.
  • Store — Exchange — trade resource cards for a specific card you want.
  • Trade (online) — swap cards with another player; both sides must agree before anything moves.
  • Champions stay locked until you conquer a warlord's entire domain — then that warlord's champion becomes available to build with and buy.

Spending & Losing Cards

  • Store — Salvage — sell spare cards for resource cards of that card's color.
  • Buying and exchanging spend your resource cards as currency.
  • Trading gives away the cards you offer.
  • Losing a planet forfeits the resource cards it was paying you.

Resource cards are your currency — the same five-color resource cards you play in battle are what you spend in the Store, so each one you keep is both economy and buying power.

Campaign 10 / 12

The campaign is a living galaxy of five faction spokes. You conquer enemy planets by winning battles, while rival factions push back — expanding and laying siege to your worlds each galaxy turn.

War Chest (Points) — each galaxy turn your planets pay income (each planet's rating) minus upkeep (2 Points for every planet you hold beyond the first 5). Spend Points on the actions below from a planet's inspector.

Sieges — a rival can besiege a border planet you own. You have 2 galaxy turns to Defend World before the siege auto-resolves and the planet can fall. You get one assault per galaxy turn, but defending never uses it — you can always defend.

Fortify — strengthens a planet you own (max level 3) when it's besieged. The cost rises per level (4 / 8 / 12 Points) and the bonuses are cumulative:

  • Level 1 — your planet starts a defense with +5 health.
  • Level 2 — also +1 starting resource.
  • Level 3 — also +1 card in your opening hand.

Fortification is lost if the planet changes hands.

Scout (3 Points) — reveal an enemy planet's defenders so you can see what you'd face.

Garrison (5 Points) — assign one of your saved decks to defend a planet, so a siege there is fought with that deck instead of the default.

Spoils — conquering a planet adds its color's resource cards to your collection; losing one forfeits them. See Collection & Store for the full picture.
